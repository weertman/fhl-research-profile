I. Publications per year

How a publication gets into the census
Verified
FHL, or the station under an earlier name, is seen in the publication itself: an author's address, the text outside the reference list (acknowledgments, methods, funding or elsewhere), the station's own series, or dataset metadata; for a small number the publication gives only the place, and the connection rests on the context of the work (— publications).
Probable
A full-text search index (OpenAlex) finds the station's name in the paper, but no one could read the passage, mostly because the publisher's site blocked automated reading (a bot check), otherwise because of a paywall (—).
Listed by FHL
On one of FHL's own publication lists, but the connection could not be seen in the publication: it is behind a paywall or a bot check, no copy is online, or the paper does not name the station (—).
Left out
Candidates that name only the town of Friday Harbor or another organization there, mention the station in passing, in a citation or an obituary, are a whole archive volume that merely contains a station paper, or could not be confirmed at all. They stay in the full census file, each with the reason for its decision.

Every probable and listed publication was reread where possible (the verification push, technical methods); those that named FHL count as verified. Those that could not be read are not in the verified count. The best estimate counts them at the share of readable papers in their tier that named FHL, and the verified ones at the precision the audit measured (Table VII). The catalog (Table VIII) gives the reason for every record.

    How the contribution was estimated
    What is counted
    The method of the University of Washington's own economic impact reports: the spending of the station, of research done there and of its students and visitors, with its supply-chain (indirect) and household-spending (induced) effects. Construction is a one-time total. Grants acknowledged by papers, gifts and the value of discoveries are reported beside the totals and never added to them.
    Spending
    FHL publishes no budget, so its operating spending was estimated three ways and reconciled: a staff roster priced at occupation-level wages for the area (Bureau of Labor Statistics) plus university benefit rates; revenue by source; and the station's statement that about a quarter of its spending comes from donors. The result is about — a year excluding scholarships, of which — is payroll. Room, board and fees paid to FHL are counted once, in operations. Research is federal awards for work at FHL, averaged over 2022–2024, direct costs only. Students and visitors count only the spending off the station of people from outside the county: in the county and, for the state figures, on their travel through Washington (ferry, shuttles, meals and Seattle lodging), estimated from published course and visitor numbers and daily spending profiles.
    Model
    The Washington Office of Financial Management's input-output table for 2012 (52 industries) gives the state model; it is scaled to San Juan County with federal employment data. Household-spending effects in that table are about twice those of the commercial IMPLAN model, so they are calibrated per dollar of labor income to two published IMPLAN results: the university's research enterprise for the state, and the Friday Harbor ferry route for the county. Taxes use the university's IMPLAN tax ratio and the state's tourism tax ratios.
    Contribution and new money
    The contribution counts all FHL-related activity in a region; the new-money impact counts only the part paid for from outside it.
    Ranges
    The model has — inputs; most carry a low, central and high value and are varied together, while the rest are fixed shares, mappings or memo values. The stated range is the 10th to 90th percentile of — random draws; most ranges are skewed upward.
    Dollars
    2024 dollars (CPI-U). Grant amounts are as awarded unless marked otherwise.

    The publication census

    Search and screening.—Every search used one list of the station's names: Friday Harbor Laboratories (and Laboratory, Labs, Lab, Marine Laboratory, "Harbour"), Puget Sound Marine Station, Puget Sound Biological Station, UW Oceanographic Laboratories, and the citation forms of the station's own series. Most searches also ran plain "Friday Harbor", which also finds the town and its other organizations; screening removed those. The bare acronym "FHL" was never searched (it is also a human gene). — AI agents each searched one family of free sources (PubMed and PubMed Central, Europe PMC, Crossref, eight data repositories, UW ResearchWorks and other thesis and report repositories, archive.org scans including the Biodiversity Heritage Library, FHL's own publication lists recovered from the Internet Archive, and news) and wrote a rerunnable script; a script searched OpenAlex; and Claude exported — Web of Science records through the UW Libraries in a browser, after the compiler signed in (Fig. 31, row 1). — more agent looked for gaps, and — more filled them. Rows were joined into one candidate when they shared a DOI or a PubMed, PubMed Central or OpenAlex identifier, or when their titles matched closely (a fuzzy similarity of at least 93 of 100), their years differed by at most one and their first authors shared a surname. Written rules decided the clear cases. AI agents judged the rest against a written screening guide that lists the station's names and the common false positives (the town, the ferry, other Friday Harbor organizations, Seattle-campus oceanography, bound volumes that merely contain a station paper). For each record the agents noted the kind of connection, where FHL appears, the type, a confidence, a reason and a quotation of the evidence. A validator had to pass every batch file: every record once, only allowed values, a reason given. Where two judgments disagree, the closer reading wins (a written rule, then AI screening, then calibration review, then a reading of the paper itself), and an audit verdict replaces a field only where the auditor could verify it. The searches ran on —.

    Where FHL appears.—Section II pools the evidence into five kinds: an author address naming the station (whether or not the text names it too); text only, with the addresses read and none naming FHL, split into the acknowledgments and the rest (methods, title, abstract, figures); text where the author addresses could not be seen; and other evidence (the station's own series, dataset records, records not readable in full). Older papers often print the author's address at the end of the article or in a first-page footnote, so a second pass read the addresses of the — verified publications whose addresses had never been seen: — give FHL as an address, in — the addresses were read and none is FHL (visitors typically gave their home university), and — could not be seen.

    Evidence tiers.—Verified: FHL was seen in the record (an author address, a passage of text, the station's own series, or dataset metadata). Probable: a full-text index names the lab, but the passage could not be read, usually because the publisher's site blocked automated reading (a bot check) and otherwise mostly because of a paywall. Listed by FHL: on an FHL-maintained publication list but not independently confirmed, either because the paper could not be read or because it does not name the station; the audit found an FHL connection in only about a third, so these are not counted as verified.

    Audit.—A stratified random sample of — decisions, drawn from every tier and from the excluded and unverified records, was re-checked by separate AI agents told to try to prove each decision wrong from the publication itself; a second agent adjudicated every disagreement. A further — records found only on FHL's own lists were judged by one agent, without adjudication (— audited in all, — of them read in full text). Precision is the share confirmed, weighted by stratum size, with Wilson 95% intervals (Table VII). The best estimate counts verified records at their audited precision. Probable and listed records that could not be read count at the share of their tier that named FHL among the papers the verification push could read (at the audit precision where fewer than 20 were read). AI agents, not people, did the audit; a human check of a sample would strengthen it.

    Recall.—FHL's own publication lists serve as an independent sample of FHL work. For each decade, recall is the share of listed papers that the database searches found on their own (Fig. 26). Listed papers that the searches missed make up the "Listed by FHL" tier.

    Types and citations.—Publication types come from each source's metadata, corrected by the screening agents, and are grouped into seven categories for Fig. 13. Citations are OpenAlex cited-by counts at retrieval, with Crossref, Europe PMC or DataCite counts where OpenAlex had none; other databases give different numbers. The citation rate in Fig. 17 divides a paper's citations by its age in years, counting the year of publication as one.

    How people, mentoring links and profiles were found, in detail

    The economic model

    Design.—Section VIII follows the structure of the University of Washington's own economic impact reports (Parker Strategy Group, 2024, for fiscal 2023): four spending sources (operations, research, students and visitors), with construction reported separately; direct, indirect and induced effects; and five measures (output, jobs supported and sustained, labor income, value added, and state and local taxes), each for the contribution of all FHL activity and for the new-money impact of spending paid for from outside the region. No IMPLAN license and no FHL records were used: every input is public.

    Regional models.—The state model is the Washington Office of Financial Management's 2012 input-output table (52 industries), rebuilt from OFM's published tables and checked against OFM's own impact worksheets. The county model scales it to San Juan County with the Flegg location quotient, computed from 2024 county and state employment by industry (Bureau of Labor Statistics, Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages), with the county's hospital and FHL's own jobs filled in where the published county file has gaps. OFM's household closure gives induced effects about twice those of IMPLAN per dollar of labor income, so induced effects are calibrated to IMPLAN results published for UW's research enterprise (state) and for the Washington State Ferries route to Friday Harbor (county). Jobs per dollar are brought from 2012 to 2024 by a blend of wage growth and consumer prices.

    Inputs.—FHL's operating spending was estimated three ways: a staff roster built from public counts and priced at occupation-level wages for the area (BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics) with UW's benefit rates; revenue by source (state and university funds, room, board and fees at published rates, endowment payout and gifts); and FHL's published statement that almost a quarter of its spending came from donors. The three agree within about $0.5 million. Research spending is the federal awards for work at FHL, spread evenly over each award's period and averaged over fiscal 2022–2024. Student and visitor spending off the station counts only people from outside the county, from published course enrollments, visitor numbers and daily spending profiles. Construction is the 2015–2024 projects found in the regents' minutes, NSF records and state capital budgets. Double counting is avoided by counting room, board and fees once (in operations), leaving scholarships out as transfers, and keeping the university's overhead on grants and the departmental pay of resident faculty as memo lines.

    Uncertainty.—Each of the — inputs has a stated basis, and those that are uncertain carry a low, central and high value. The ranges quoted are the 10th to 90th percentiles of — Monte Carlo draws; Fig. 20 moves one input at a time. Three independent reviews (economic method, the provenance of every input, and the regional treatment against peer marine stations) were answered before the results were fixed. The output per direct dollar, 1.81 in the state and 1.36 in the county, sits beside published values for peer marine laboratories (1.83 for Bigelow Laboratory in Maine, 1.53 for Duke's marine laboratory in its county).

    Limits.—The input-output table dates from 2012 and the county model is a non-survey regionalization. FHL's budget, payroll, purchases and user records are not published, so the station's own figures are estimates; with the station's accounts the model reruns in seconds. FHL's 2026 rates, its 2024 scholarship total and its donor share (published in 2023) were read from Internet Archive copies of its pages. No value is assigned to discoveries made at FHL, to grants held at visitors' home institutions or to the ecosystem services of the reserves. The full method and the model's inputs, results and sensitivities are published with the data; the code is kept with the census files. AI agents (Claude Opus 5.5) gathered the sources, built the model and reviewed it in November 2026.
